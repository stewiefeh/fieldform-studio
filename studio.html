The studio

Thirteen people. One drawing set at a time.

Fieldform was founded in 2011 on a stubborn idea: a small studio that stays involved from the first sketch to the final punch list does better buildings than a big one that hands your project between departments.

How it started

A kitchen table, a bad builder, and a better plan.

Mara Ellison and Daniel Okafor started Fieldform after watching a well-funded house project fall apart in construction documents. The design was fine. The drawings were not something a builder could price without guessing, and the client paid for that twice.

The fix became the studio's spine: draw what you will build, price what you draw, and stay on site. Thirteen years later the tools changed, that part did not.

Today the studio works across Oregon, Washington, and the Northeast, with a second bench in the Hudson Valley for the residential work that keeps us honest.

Rule one

No rendering lies.

Every image we show is buildable at the budget attached to it. If a view costs forty thousand dollars, the image says so.

Rule two

Site visits weekly.

Someone from the design team walks the site every week during construction. Non-negotiable in every contract.

Rule three

Fixings first.

Shade, orientation, and airflow before any gadget. A well-placed window outperforms a wall of controls.

Rule four

Finish the punch list.

We do not close a project at practical completion. We close it when the building works the way the drawings promised.
