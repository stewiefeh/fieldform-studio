Ashgrove House

Completed
2025
Area
340 m²
Scope
Full architecture and interiors
Recognition
AIA Honor Award, 2025

The brief was a big house. The answer was a small one.

The Kellers arrived with a program for 500 square meters and a site that could barely carry 300 without losing its orchard. Rather than flatten the hill, we spent the first month counting how the family actually lived, room by room, hour by hour. It turned out they lived in three places: the kitchen table, the study, and the porch.

So the house is those three rooms, done properly, plus the bedrooms that have to exist. The plan is a pinwheel around a covered court that works as an outdoor room nine months of the year. Everything else, the formal dining room, the guest wing they never used in the last house, went back to the program's cutting room floor, and the budget followed it.

Structure is board-formed concrete for the two upper volumes, sitting on a glazed ground floor that almost disappears. The cantilever is not a stunt: it holds the bedroom wing out over the slope so no tree came down. Oak lines every surface a hand touches. The clients' only instruction at handover was to promise we would take their call when the nursery needed converting. We did, last spring. It took one afternoon.

Key moves

  • Pinwheel plan around a covered court
  • Two concrete volumes, one glazed plinth
  • No trees removed on the building side of the orchard
  • Oak linings on every tactile surface
  • Passive stack ventilation, no mechanical cooling
